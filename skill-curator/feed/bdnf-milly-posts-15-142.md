How to sync your Circadian Rhythm

Your body runs on multiple clocks. Your body does not run on a single clock. Instead, it uses a hierarchy of clocks. The master clock in your brain, the suprachiasmatic nucleus, receives light signals from your eyes and coordinates the timing of nearly every process in your body. Almost every organ also has its own local clock, including the liver, heart, muscles, and digestive system. Circadian rhythm: A roughly 24-hour cycle that regulates sleep, wakefulness, hormone release, and body temperature. Ultradian rhythms: Shorter cycles that repeat multiple times a day, such as 90-minute sleep stages. Infradian rhythms: Longer cycles that last more than a day, such as the menstrual cycle.

When Clocks Fall Out of Sync. When your clocks are not properly synchronized, the timing of your body becomes misaligned. This can happen with irregular sleep schedules, late-night light exposure, shift work, jet lag, or inconsistent meal times. The effects are more than just feeling tired. Misalignment is linked to reduced attention, slower reaction time, impaired memory, poor decision making, and increased risk of metabolic issues. Irregular sleep: Disrupts the master clock.
