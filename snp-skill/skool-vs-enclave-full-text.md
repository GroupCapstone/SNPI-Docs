This is why Alex Hormozi's Skool sucks. Skool bases its progression off of engagement, any sort of engagement, which poses a decent community in their community is the only way to level up and move on to the next modules. And yeah, that's cool if you want more of a cult-like community, but if you genuinely care about making sure your coaching gives real results to real people, then it's just not for you.

That's why I'm building Enclave. Enclave is built around the philosophy that we actually want people to get value out of buying a course. And we want you to know that your course is working.

Now two of the main ways we do this is, one, we gate this behind genuine progression, not engagement.

1. Gated Progression System
So number one, a quiz or a knowledge check allows you to grade it yourself or an AI to make sure that they actually understand the insights that you're trying to instill within your clients. Creators define mastery requirements per module. Students cannot advance until they have met the criteria: passing a knowledge check, uploading proof-of-work (a screenshot, photo, metric, or short video), or completing required daily inputs. This is fundamentally different from Skool's gamification, which gates content based on engagement points (likes, comments). Enclave gates content based on demonstrated competence.

2. Proof-of-Work Uploads
And secondly is the proof-of-work uploads. Now the proof-of-work is a double-edged sword. Not only does it give you real physical evidence of your teaching working on your clients, it also gives you free marketing material and testimonials to share with the world and get more clients. At key milestones, students upload verifiable proof of their progress. This serves a dual purpose: it gates progression (students must submit proof to unlock the next module) and it automatically builds a library of testimonials and wins. Creators no longer have to chase students for social proof. The platform collects it as a natural byproduct of the learning process. With student permission, these uploads can be formatted into social-ready content for the creator's marketing.
