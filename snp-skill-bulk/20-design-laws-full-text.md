# 20 Design Laws to Give Claude
Spoken text from the video, recorded 2026-11-25.

1. Aesthetic-usability effect
Pretty designs feel easier to use.

2. Law of proximity
Things close together look like a group.

3. Von Restorff effect
The odd one stands out.

4. Zeigarnik effect
Unfinished tasks stay on your mind.

5. Tesler's law
Some work can't be removed, so let the app do it.

6. Law of Prägnanz
Simple shapes are quicker to read.

7. Serial position effect
We remember the first and last items best.

8. Progressive disclosure
Show the basics first, the rest when asked.

9. Law of common region
Things in the same box look like a group.

10. Goal-gradient effect
The closer the goal, the harder we push.

11. Law of similarity
Things that look alike seem to work alike.

12. Recognition over recall
Picking from a list is easier than remembering.

13. Pareto principle
20% of the features get 80% of the use.

14. Occam's razor
If both work, pick the one with fewer parts.

15. Parkinson's law
Work fills the time you give it.

16. Uniform connectedness
Things joined by a line look related.

17. Paradox of the active user
People skip the instructions and dive right in.

18. Cognitive load
Your brain can only juggle so much at once.

19. Default effect
Most people keep what's already selected.

20. Peak-end rule
The big moment and the end decide how it felt.
