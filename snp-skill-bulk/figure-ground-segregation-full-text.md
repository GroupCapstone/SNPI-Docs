# Figure-Ground Segregation
Text from the video, recorded 2026-11-25.

Two regions share the same edge, but your brain picks one as the figure. Watch which region pops forward. Now watch it flip. Size and symmetry are equal here. Only convexity differs. The brain combines several cues at once: Size, Symmetry, Convexity. But convexity often outweighs symmetry when they compete. Kanizsa & Gerbino, 1976.

Figure-ground segregation: the visual system splits every scene into one figure and its background. This process is called figure-ground segregation.
