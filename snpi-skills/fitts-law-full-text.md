Fitts' Law
Your biggest button can still be the slowest one to hit. How fast you hit it depends on how far away it is. Farther targets take longer, while bigger targets take less time. Distance and width combine into one difficulty score. Subjects repeatedly tapped targets at different distances and widths, and their movement times were measured. The harder it is to hit, the higher the score. That difficulty score closely predicts their movement time. This is Fitts' Law. MT = a + b · log2(2D/W). Movement time depends on distance and target width.
